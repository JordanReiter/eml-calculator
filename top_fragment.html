The Two-Button Calculator

This mini-project was inspired by the Numberphile video {{video_title}} featuring Dr James Grime, which explores a paper by Andrzej Odrzywołek describing how to reduce all possible calculations to a single operator.

In the video, Dr. Grime describes an imaginary scenario where you are in a test and one of the buttons on your scientific calculators has broken. The question then becomes: what are the minimum number of buttons you need in order to make all possible calculations?

Turns out the number is just 4:

  • 1
  • −
  • ln (natural logarithm function)
  • exp function (or ℯn)

Grime goes on to explain that using a special operator described by Odrzywołek, we can switch to just 2 buttons:

  • 1
  • EML (exponent minus ln), a special operator that combines the operators above:
    EML(x,y) -> ℯx − ln y
